A home rarely has one mood. The entrance welcomes people, the living room gathers them, and the bedroom lets everyone rest. Fragrance works best when it follows what each room is for.

## Entrance and hallway

The hallway is the first thing a guest notices. Choose something clear and recognisable, and keep it consistent so the house has a signature. A reed diffuser works well here because it scents continuously without any attention.

## Living spaces

Living rooms are larger and busier, so they need more diffusion. A scent machine or several diffusers placed away from open windows give you steady coverage. Keep the scent in the background so people notice the room, not the fragrance.

## Bedroom

Keep the bedroom softer. Scent the room before you rest rather than all night, and allow fresh air in the morning.

## Bathroom

Humidity carries fragrance well. A small diffuser is usually enough in a compact bathroom.

## A note on intensity

Start lighter than you think you need. You can always build up, and a scent you live with every day should never tire you.
